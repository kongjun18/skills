# Profile: research group talk

An ongoing research project presented to the research group, on its way to a paper. The talk argues why the problem matters and what question the work asks; it is a dry run of the paper's argument.

## Audience

Researchers in the group: they know the field broadly, not this project's details or its internal names. Every slide must make sense to someone who sees only it and the slides before it.

## Structure

Follow the paper:

| Part | Role | Typical slides |
|---|---|---|
| Title | title, subtitle, authors, affiliation; nothing else | 1 |
| Background | the case existing work already makes, as a logic chain of accepted facts | 3-5 |
| Motivation | the gap that calls for this work: what existing work leaves unexplained or unexplored, with its evidence and the related work | 1-2 |
| Research question(s) | the question, and for each sub-question the direction it points to | 1 |
| Implications / directions | what changes if the answer is as hypothesized; for whom | 1 |
| Summary | three takeaways | 1 |
| Backup | references, extra detail no claim depends on | as needed |

- Background and Motivation do not overlap. Background slides state accepted facts, mostly from other papers; the motivation slide is the first place the gap appears.
- Check the chain between consecutive background slides for a missing step.
- No outline or section-outline slides unless asked; the part tag shows where the talk is.

## Content rules

- Directions, not results: for ongoing work, research-question and implication slides give directions and hypotheses, labelled as such. No early answers or numbers unless the user asks. Early signs, if any, go in the notes or are explicitly marked "early sign".
- No design content (formats, runtimes, controllers, cost models) when the user excludes it.
- Prefer other papers' data in Background and Motivation; cite on the slide; add a References backup slide with the numbered list.
- Novelty and gap claims are checked against related work and worded carefully: "unexplored", "partly explained", "not studied for this workload".
- The motivation slide shows related work as a small table: work, what it studies, what it leaves open.

## Defaults

- Length: ask; about 20 minutes is common. Estimate 100-110 words per minute of notes.
- Title slide: group-meeting title, the talk's subtitle, authors and affiliation lines from the project override.
- Template: the project's, else the bundled Google Slides default.

## Extra checks

- [ ] Each Background slide states a fact existing work established, with a citation.
- [ ] The motivation slide is the first to state the gap, and the gap is checked against related work.
- [ ] Research-question slides contain no results or design.
- [ ] Every reference cited on a slide is in the References slide.
